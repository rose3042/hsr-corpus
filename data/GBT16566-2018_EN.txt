Terms for railway tunnel

1 Scope

This standard specifies the basic terms and definitions for railway tunnels.
This standard is applicable to the planning. survey. design. construction. operation, research, train-

ing and other aspects of railway tunnel engineering, and may also be referenced by other under-
ground projects.

2 General terms for tunnel

2.1
railway tunnel

structure built underground or underwater, with tracks laid for the railway rolling stock as well as the
machines and tools that can move on the tracks to pass through

2.2
extra-long tunnel

tunnel with a total length of more than 10 000 m

2.3
long tunnel

tunnel with a total length within the range of 3 000 m to 10 000 m

2.4
medium length tunnel

tunnel with a total length within the range of 500 m to 3 000 m

2.5
short length tunnel

tunnel with a total length of 500 m or less

tunnel with one track laid

2.9
double-track tunnel

tunnel with two tracks laid

2. 10
multi-track tunnel

tunnel with more than two tracks laid

2.11
multi-arch tunnel

tunnel consisting of two arches seperated by a partitional wall

2.12
neighboring tunnels

two tunnels with small distance in between that cannot be considered as independent tunnels

2.13

3 Surrounding rock

3.1

tunnel surrounding rock
rock and soil mass affecting the tunnel trunk within a certain range around the tunnel

3.2
rock quality designation; R.Q.D

ratio of the sum of the lengths of over-10 cm rock core sections that are taken continuously in the
borehole in the same layer by using a 75 mm diameter diamond bit to the total drilling footage of the
rock stratum, which is expressed in percentage

3.3
integration coefficient of rock mass

ratio of the square of the longitudinal wave velocity in the rock mass to the square of the longitudinal
wave velocity in the rock block that constitutes such rock mass; the larger the integration coeffi-
cient, the more integrated the rock mass is. that is. the fewer the cracks

3.4
tunnel surrounding rock classification

classification of surrounding rock by stability according to the main indexes such as the integrity of
rock and soil mass and the hardness of rock

3.5
pressure of surrounding rock

pressure exerted on the supporting or lining structure due to deformation or relaxation of surround-
ing rock after tunnel excavation, also called formation pressure

GB/T 16566—2018

3.6
loosening pressure

pressure exerted on the supporting or lining structure by the weight of the surrounding rock at a cer-
tain height due to the loosening of the surrounding rock above the tunnel after tunnel excavation

3.7
deformation pressure

pressure exerted on the supporting structure due to the deformation of surrounding rock after tunnel
excavation

3.8
rock-self stability time

time for the surrounding rock itself to reach continuous stability without any support after excava-
tion and exposure

3.9
surrounding rock reinforcement; ground stabilization

engineering measure to improve the engineering characteristics of surrounding rock. increase its
overall strength and stability or reduce its permeability

3.10
initial in-situ stress field

in-situ stress formed in rock mass due to self-weight and tectonic movement of rock under natural
conditions

3.11
elastic rebound resistance

formation reaction force caused by lining deformation towards surrounding rock

3.12
natural arch

self-supporting arch formed by surrounding rock above the tunnel to maintain stability of rock and
soil after excavation of the tunnel on the assumption that the surrounding rock pressure is independ-
ent of the buried depth of the stratum

GB/T 16566—2018

3. 13
heading slope

cut slope above the tunnel portal

4 Survey and design

4.1
survey outside the tunnel; surface survey

general term for survey conducted outside the tunnel. including large scale ground topographic map-
ping in the design stage, and ground plane and elevation control survey. tunnel portal alignment sur-
vey, ground and underground connection survey in the construction stage

4.2
survey inside the tunnel; tunnel survey

general term for survey conducted in the tunnel, including the elevation control survey of the ground
plane, the traverse and leveling survey in the tunnel, the connection survey between the ground and
the tunnel. the construction setting out of the portal structures in the construction stage. and the
setting out survey of the centerline. elevation and tunnel cross section during excavation

4.3
through survey

survey to ensure that different excavation faces at each construction portal are connected with pre-
determined accuracy and that each construction structure has the specified accuracy

4.4
setting horizontal point at portal

horizontal control point set near the portal for controlling the traverse survey in the tunnel during
tunnel survey

4.5
environmental survey

survey on the impact of tunnel construction on the environment along the line

4.6

project

4.7
engineering exploration

engineering characteristics. including geophysical exploration, simple exploration (excavation pros-
pecting, Luoyang shovel exploration, small spiral drilling, light dynamic penetration, etc. ), static
cone penetration and drilling

4.8
special rock and soil

rock and soil mass with special mineral composition and structure. special physical, mechanical and
chemical properties and affecting engineering geological conditions, which mainly includes loess, ex-

salt rock (gypsum, anhydrite. rock salt, natural alkali. mirabilite) , etc.

4.9

prehensively analyze the distribution characteristics of natural or artificial physical fields, and to de-
tect the geological body or geological structure

4.10

starting point of the buffer structure for tunnel with buffer structure

4.11
buried depth; depth of tunnel

vertical distance from the rail top to the ground surface in the tunnel

5 Railway tunnel construction method

5.1

under-cutting method; tunnelling method

tunnel construction method in which excavation and lining installation are performed entirely under-
ground, mainly including shield method, tunnel boring machine method, drilling and blasting meth-
od, etc.

5.2

cut and cover method; open-cut method

tunnel construction method in which the ground surface is excavated first. then the tunnel lining
structure is built, and finally the earth and rock are backfilled and the ground is restored

5.3
shield method

tunnelling method for tunnel construction by using shield machine for excavation, mucking. lining
and other operations

GB/T 16566—2018

5.4
tunnel-boring machine method; TBM method

method of tunnel excavation using large and efficient tunnel construction machinery with multiple
functions. such as tunnelling (mechanical cutting of rock), mucking and support

5.5
immersed tunnelling method

construction method in which a structural frame is constructed in sections on the ground and then
gradually sunk by its own weight to form an underground structure

5.6
freezing method

construction method in which the surrounding rock is frozen to reach a certain strength before exca-
vation and then thawed after support is made in case it is difficult to form the tunnel due to poor
surrounding rock conditions (such as weak ground or water bearing sand layer) during tunnel excava-
tion

5.7
new austrian tunnelling method; NATM

tunnel construction method that adopts rock bolt, shotcrete and other surrounding rock support and
reinforcement means as well as reasonable excavation method, and guides the design and construc-
tion and controls the deformation of surrounding rock by monitoring measurement. so that the sur-
rounding rock becomes a part of the supporting system to give full play to its self-supporting ability
and maintain its stability

5.8
drilling and blasting method

tunnel construction method of drilling holes in rock and soil and loading explosives for blasting exca-
vation

6 Tunnel construction machinery

6.1
